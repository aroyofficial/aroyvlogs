A day-by-day Kolkata Durga Puja itinerary covering the pandals planned for the 2026 festival circuit.

THE PLAN

Our Puja Parikrama

Durga Puja in Kolkata is best experienced one neighbourhood at a time. For 2026, we are planning a five-day pandal-hopping journey across the city, combining iconic theme pujas, traditional community celebrations and some of the city's most atmospheric neighbourhoods.

The current route is built as a connected city circuit rather than a collection of isolated stops. Across the five days, we move through North Kolkata's heritage lanes, South Kolkata's major puja belt, Central Kolkata and the eastern neighbourhoods around Lake Town, Salt Lake, Newtown and Dum Dum Park. Metro, local trains, buses, cabs and short walks are used to keep each day's movement practical.

Across the five planned days, we are targeting 80 pandals: 14 on Chaturthi, 21 on Panchami, 16 on Shashthi, 8 on Saptami and 21 on Navami. The goal is to cover a wide range of neighbourhood pujas without turning the trip into a race, leaving room to eat, walk, photograph and actually enjoy the pandals along the way.

UNESCO HERITAGE

Tradition & History

THE ROOTS

From Zamindari Courtyards to the Streets

Durga Puja's history in Bengal traces back to late medieval times, originating as grand household celebrations hosted by wealthy landowners and aristocrats (Bonedi Barir Puja). By the early 20th century, the festival transitioned into public, community-funded events (Sarbojanin), turning the streets of Kolkata into a massive, democratic art gallery accessible to everyone, regardless of class or creed.

In 2021, UNESCO inscribed Kolkata's Durga Puja on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, recognizing its unique blend of art, community bonding, and craft traditions.

THE ARTISTRY

Crafting the Divine & the Ephemeral

At the heart of the festival is the extraordinary craftsmanship. Months before the autumn festival, clay modelers in Kumartuli sculpt exquisite idols of Goddess Durga and her children from sacred Ganga clay. Parallelly, architects and decorators build elaborate, thematic temporary structures (pandals) using bamboo, fabric, wood, and recycled materials, showcasing breathtaking creativity that is dismantled after the five-day carnival.

The air during the festival is thick with the sound of traditional Dhak drums, the aroma of burning incense (dhunuchi), and local delicacies that define Kolkata's street food culture.

North Kolkata now forms one of the largest heritage-heavy sections of the plan, running through Tala, Bagbazar, Kumartuli, Ahiritola, Maniktala, Hatibagan and Sovabazar. The route also reaches Chalta Bagan and surrounding neighbourhood lanes, making this circuit especially rich in traditional community pujas and older parts of the city.

Central Kolkata links the northern and southern circuits through Jorasanko, Chakraberia, Central and College Street. These sections are deliberately connected with short walks and public transport so the itinerary can keep moving without repeatedly crossing the city.

South Kolkata remains the broadest day-to-day footprint, stretching across Ballygunge, Garia, Santoshpur, Kalighat, Chetla, Haridevpur, Behala and Thakurpukur. The route mixes major landmark pujas with smaller neighbourhood celebrations and several planned food stops.

Finally, the East & North-East circuit heads through Lake Town and Beliaghata into Salt Lake and Newtown before finishing around Dum Dum Park and Arjunpur. This section uses a combination of buses, metro and cabs to connect otherwise spread-out eastern clusters efficiently.

GUIDE FOR OTHERS

How to Follow this Parikrama

If you are planning to follow or adapt these itineraries for your own Puja walk or vlog, here are a few practical tips designed to make the experience smooth and enjoyable:

⏱️ Timing is Everything

Our itineraries start around 9:00 AM or 10:00 AM to balance crowd levels. For the best photography and shortest lines, start early in the morning. If you want to experience the dramatic lighting, plan your walks for late evening, but prepare for heavy crowds.

🚇 Transit Strategy

Skip ticket lines by getting a Metro Smart Card. When transitioning between zones, use the metro network where practical. For short hops or late-night transfers, use a bus, auto-rickshaw, cab or local yellow taxi.

📸 Vlogging & Etiquette

Keep your gear light. A smartphone or a compact mirrorless camera on a gimbal works best in crowded lanes. Be respectful of worshippers inside the inner sanctum of the pandals where photography is sometimes restricted.

🍲 Pandal Food Crawl

Puja and food are inseparable. The route already includes planned lunch stops, while temporary food stalls near major pandals are useful for quick breaks between longer walking sections.
